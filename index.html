🦁 Meet the Animals

Every animal has a story. Read about our residents, their natural habitats, their amazing adaptations and the challenges they face in the wild. The photos load from Wikipedia when an internet connection is available, except for the blue-tongued skink, which uses our own picture.

African Savannah Elephant

The African savannah elephant is the largest land animal on Earth. Its trunk can smell, breathe, pick up food, drink water and greet other elephants. Huge ears help release heat in the African sun, while tusks can help with digging and moving objects. Elephants live in close family groups and communicate using sounds that can travel over long distances. These intelligent giants need large areas to find food and water.

Featured Animal

Eastern Blue-Tongued Lizard

This Australian lizard is famous for its bright blue tongue, which it flashes to surprise predators. Its sturdy body and short legs help it search for fruit, flowers, insects and other food on the ground. If threatened, it may hiss and flatten its body to look larger. Blue-tongued skinks are well adapted to life among leaf litter, logs and low vegetation. Their blue tongues are a brilliant defence display, helping them appear more intimidating to potential predators. They are ground-dwelling reptiles that use their strong jaws to eat a varied diet. With proper care, blue-tongued skinks can live for many years.

Reptile · Special Photo

Goliath Birdeater

Native to the rainforests of northern South America, the Goliath birdeater is one of the heaviest spiders in the world. Despite its name, it mainly eats insects and other small animals rather than birds. It often shelters in a burrow and uses sensitive hairs to detect movement nearby. Its enormous size and powerful fangs make it an impressive predator of the forest floor.

Invertebrate

Brazilian Wandering Spider

This active spider hunts at night instead of building a web to catch prey. It wanders through tropical habitats searching for insects and other small animals. Some wandering spiders have medically significant venom, so they must be observed safely and never handled. Their hunting behaviour makes them different from spiders that wait quietly for prey to become trapped in a web.

Invertebrate

Brazilian Salmon Pink Tarantula

This large tarantula comes from Brazil and is known for the pinkish hairs covering its body and legs. It hunts insects and other small animals, using its strong legs to capture prey. Specialised hairs help it sense vibrations around its burrow. Like many tarantulas, it spends much of its time on the ground and relies on camouflage and patience to survive.

Invertebrate

Southern White Rhinoceros

This enormous herbivore spends much of its day grazing on grass. Its broad mouth is specially shaped to crop short vegetation, and its powerful body helps it travel across open grasslands. Rhino horns are made of keratin, the same material found in human hair and fingernails. Poaching and habitat pressures have threatened rhinos, making conservation and protected areas essential to their future.

Mammal

Red-Eyed Tree Frog

This colourful rainforest amphibian has brilliant red eyes, a green body and orange feet. It is mainly active at night, when it hunts insects among the leaves. Its bright colours can startle predators, while its green back helps it blend into leafy surroundings when resting. Specialised toe pads help it cling to plants as it climbs through its humid tropical habitat.

Amphibian

American Bullfrog

One of North America's largest frogs, the American bullfrog lives around ponds, lakes and wetlands. Males make a deep call that sounds a little like a cow mooing. It waits near the water for insects and other suitable prey, then uses powerful hind legs to leap away from danger. Its life cycle begins as an egg, develops through a tadpole stage and eventually becomes an adult frog.

Amphibian

Brown-Throated Sloth

This slow-moving mammal lives high in Central and South American forest canopies. Curved claws help it hang upside down while it eats leaves and rests. Sloths conserve energy by moving slowly, and algae growing in their fur can sometimes give them a greenish appearance. Their specialised limbs and gripping claws make them excellent climbers, perfectly suited to life in the trees.

Mammal

Boa Constrictor

Boa constrictors are powerful snakes found in Central and South America. Their forked tongues collect scent particles, helping them explore their surroundings and locate prey. Instead of using venom, they wrap their muscular bodies around prey. Their patterned scales provide camouflage among branches and leaf litter. After catching prey, a boa can swallow it whole using its flexible jaws.

Reptile

Cloudless Sulphur Butterfly

This bright yellow butterfly flies through warm, sunny habitats and visits flowers for nectar. Its caterpillars feed on particular host plants before forming a chrysalis and emerging as adults. Its fast, fluttering flight can make it look like a tiny flash of sunshine. As it visits flowers, it can also help transfer pollen between plants.

Butterfly House

European Peacock Butterfly

The European peacock butterfly has large eyespots on its wings that can startle potential predators. When it closes its wings, the dark underside helps it blend into its surroundings. Adults feed on nectar, while caterpillars commonly feed on nettles. Its striking markings make it one of Europe's most recognisable butterflies and a colourful addition to the Butterfly House.

Butterfly House

Malabar Rose Butterfly

The Malabar rose is a striking swallowtail butterfly associated with the Indian subcontinent. Its dark wings feature pale markings and red details, making it easy to recognise. Like other butterflies, it begins life as a caterpillar, then forms a chrysalis before emerging as an adult. Its life cycle shows the remarkable transformation known as complete metamorphosis.

Butterfly House

Menelaus Blue Morpho Butterfly

This tropical butterfly is famous for its brilliant blue wings. The colour is created by the way microscopic scales reflect light, rather than simply by blue pigment. Underneath, its wings are brown and patterned, helping it hide among leaves when resting. As it flies through the forest, flashes of bright blue can make it look almost magical.

Butterfly House

Kinger's Butterfly (Monarch)

Kinger's Butterfly is our TADC tribute! The real monarch butterfly has bold orange-and-black wings edged with white spots. Some monarch populations in North America undertake extraordinary migrations between breeding areas and wintering sites. Their journey can involve several generations. Like all butterflies, monarchs begin life as caterpillars before changing inside a chrysalis and emerging with wings.

TADC Tribute

Old World Swallowtail Butterfly

This large butterfly has yellow-and-black wings with small tail-like extensions on its hindwings. It lives across much of Europe and Asia, where adults visit flowers for nectar. Its caterpillars feed on plants in the carrot family before pupating. The swallowtail's elegant flight and bold wing patterns make it a standout resident of our Butterfly House.

Butterfly House

Amazonian Giant Centipede

This large centipede lives in tropical parts of South America. Its long, flattened body and many pairs of legs help it move quickly through leaf litter and crevices. It is a predator that catches insects and other small animals using modified front limbs that deliver venom. Although its appearance can be intimidating, it plays an important role in its ecosystem as a hunter.

Invertebrate

Axolotl

The axolotl is an aquatic salamander native to lakes around Mexico City. Unlike most amphibians, it usually keeps its feathery external gills and aquatic features throughout adulthood. It can regenerate certain body parts, including limbs, making it important in scientific research. Wild axolotls are critically endangered, so protecting their remaining wetland habitat is especially important.

Amphibian

Reticulated Giraffe

The reticulated giraffe is one of the tallest land animals on Earth. Its long neck helps it reach leaves high in trees, while its distinctive coat pattern breaks up its outline among branches and shadows. A long, flexible tongue helps it pull leaves from thorny plants. Giraffes often live in loose groups and can use their height to spot danger across open landscapes.

Mammal

Bengal Tiger

The Bengal tiger is a powerful big cat native to the Indian subcontinent. Its orange coat and dark stripes provide camouflage in forests and tall grass. Tigers are usually solitary hunters, relying on stealth and strength to catch prey. Every tiger has its own stripe pattern, and protecting large natural habitats is essential for the survival of wild tiger populations.

Big Cat

Capybara

The capybara is the world's largest living rodent and comes from South America. It is an excellent swimmer, with partly webbed feet and eyes, ears and nostrils positioned high on its head. Capybaras live in groups near water and graze on grasses and aquatic plants. When danger appears, they can dive underwater to escape predators.

Mammal

Hippopotamus

Hippos spend much of the day in rivers and pools, where water helps keep their enormous bodies cool. At night, they leave the water to graze on grass. Despite their bulky appearance, they can move surprisingly quickly on land and are highly territorial in water. Their eyes and nostrils sit high on their heads, allowing them to watch while mostly submerged.

Mammal

American Alligator

The American alligator lives in freshwater wetlands in the southeastern United States. Its powerful jaws, armoured body and strong tail make it a well-adapted aquatic predator. Alligators often float with only their eyes and nostrils visible above the water. They also help shape wetland ecosystems by creating small pools that can provide habitat for other wildlife.

Reptile

Aardvarks and Meerkats

The aardvark is a nocturnal African mammal with strong claws for digging into termite mounds and ant nests. Its long, sticky tongue can collect thousands of insects in a night — nature's living hoover! Meerkats live in family groups and take turns watching for predators. Both animals have special adaptations that help them survive in dry African environments.

African Wildlife

European Badgers

European badgers are mostly nocturnal mammals recognised by their black-and-white striped faces. They live in underground tunnel systems called setts, which can be used by generations of the same family. Badgers eat a varied diet including earthworms, insects, fruit and other foods. Their powerful front claws help them dig their homes and search for meals beneath the soil.

Mammal

West African Lion

The West African lion is a rare lion population from West Africa. Lions are social big cats that may live in groups called prides, although their social structure varies by region. They hunt large herbivores and often rest during the heat of the day. Habitat loss and conflict with people have put these lions at serious risk, making conservation and protected areas vital to their future.

Big Cat

Grizzly Bears

Grizzly bears are powerful brown bears found in parts of North America. Their shoulder hump supports strong muscles that help them dig for roots, insects and other foods. They are omnivores, eating berries, plants, fish and small animals depending on the season. Before winter, they may eat large amounts to build fat reserves. Despite their size, grizzlies are excellent swimmers and can run quickly.

🚤 The Final Ride

Climb aboard for one last journey through the wild! Our riverboat takes you past five incredible animal stops before reaching the grand finale.

1. Addax

The addax is a desert antelope with long, twisted horns and pale fur. Its wide hooves help it travel across sand, and it can survive in extremely dry environments by getting much of its water from food.

2. African Buffalo

The African buffalo is a powerful grazer that lives in herds. Its curved horns provide protection, and its strong social bonds can help members of the herd respond to danger. These animals are an important part of African savannah ecosystems.

3. African Penguin

The African penguin lives along the coasts of southern Africa. Its flippers make it an agile swimmer, helping it chase fish underwater. Distinctive black-and-white markings provide camouflage in the sea, while its loud calls help penguins recognise their mates and chicks.

4. Alpine Ibex

The Alpine ibex is a wild goat that lives in the steep mountains of Europe. Its specialised hooves provide grip on rocky surfaces, allowing it to climb slopes that seem almost impossible. Males grow enormous curved horns that become more pronounced as they age.

5. Polar Bears — The Grand Finale!

The polar bear is the largest living bear species and is specially adapted to Arctic life. Thick fur and a layer of body fat help it stay warm in freezing conditions. Its large paws help it walk over snow and swim through icy water. Polar bears depend heavily on sea ice for hunting seals, so the loss of Arctic sea ice is a major threat.
